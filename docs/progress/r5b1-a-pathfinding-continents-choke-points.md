**In progress** (`ee_sim::astar`, formerly `ee_astar`). Branch `rust-ai-path2` (PORT_LEARNINGS "R5b.1, round 4: the continent manager's data in Rust"): the continent manager's data and most of its logic are Rust. `EEAIContinentManager` holds one member, `EeAStarCmRecord` (`cm.rs`: its counters, the sector grid that was `EEAIPathSectorRegionInformation`, the four tile grids, with the tables behind it: the serial table, the eight kinds' tables by id, the pending sector tiles, the shore lists, the free ids). Every area kind's own members are a record, its class's private base (`area_kinds.rs`: path area, choke point, forest, hill, rectangle area; `continent.rs` with the continents' neighbours; `sector_rec.rs`: the path sectors and sub sectors with their links and neighbours). Over the records, Rust has the saved block (save 0.263 byte for byte, `cm_io.rs`), the CNTR record and CNTQ's digest, the searches through the sectors (`FindContinent`, `FindPathSubSector`), the queries (`cm_query.rs`: the area lookups by tile, the choke point lists, connections, largest and random areas, `InSameArea`, `GetContinent`), `RemoveForestTile`, `RepairPathArea` and `GetAdjacentAreaID` (`cm_repair.rs`), the link walks' `LinkSubSectors` and `RemoveSubSectors` (`cm_links.rs`), and `CreateContinentGrid`'s store, `UpdateTileValues`, `EEAIRectangleArea::UpdateContinents` (`cm_build.rs`). The C++ registers each record's place in its class once (`EeAStarCmLayout`) and answers one host (EEAIContinentSave.cpp): `Contains`, `Create` and `RemoveTile` (their hull records), a tile's kind, wall, terrain, wood and AI value, `EEGetRandomInt`, the town or herd an area is, the region size, making and deleting the objects.

Earlier rounds (branch `rust-ai-path1`): the path manager whole in Rust (`pm.rs`, `pm_bounded.rs`, `pm_record.rs`), PMRQ, the CNTQ capture of the continent queries; before them the node storage, the search machine and every goal's hooks of every tile search, the graph searches, the continent analysis leaf by leaf, the sparse point set, the hulls and the areas' geometry.
